    How the agent scores a player

    1. Time on ice & power-play unit. Expected even-strength minutes are a weighted average of the last games, pulled toward the season average. Expected power-play minutes are the player's recent share of his team's PP time times the PP time teams get on an average night. PP1 = at least of the team's PP time, PP2 = at least . Those minutes are multiplied by his points per 60 at even strength and on the power play (this season + last + two seasons ago at half weight, shrunk toward the position average: ), blended with an expected-goals rate: on-ice expected goals for per 60 × the share of on-ice goals he gets a point on (xG data: MoneyPuck.com). Expected goals settle down faster than points, so this steadies the rates.
    2. Line combinations & matchups. Lines and defence pairs are read from the last 2 games' shift charts (who shares the most 5-on-5 ice). Linemates' even-strength scoring vs league average scales the player's even-strength rate (power , capped at ×0.85–×1.20; defencemen use the forwards they're most often on with). The matchup factors in the opponent's shots allowed per game and penalty kill (this season, blended with last season), how often they're shorthanded (more PP minutes), home ice (×1.03 / ×0.97), the second night of a back-to-back (×; on this season's games, opponent back-to-backs and time zones crossed showed no effect), and the player's history against them (shrunk with pseudo-games, capped at ±0.4 log-odds).
    3. Team total from the betting market. Each game's moneyline and total (DraftKings and FanDuel, bookmaker margin removed) are turned into expected goals for each team, pulled once a day at the first run after . The model keeps each player's share of his team's expected points (ice time, power-play unit, line), but the team's scoring level is set to the market's: every player on a team is scaled by market goals ÷ the model's own team goals (capped ×0.70–×1.40). The market already reflects the opposing goalie and defence, so for teams with a line the matchup and goalie factors mostly shape who scores rather than how much.
    4. Blend with the market. Last, where DraftKings, FanDuel or Kalshi price the player, the model is blended with the market's no-vig probability: . The weight is refitted every run from the track record (settled pre-game forecasts), so it rises if the model starts beating the market. Without a market price, the raw probability is pulled in toward typical rates, because the raw model is overconfident. "Edge" is the blended probability minus the books' price, a realistic expected edge rather than the raw disagreement; the raw model is in each player's breakdown.
    5. Opposing goalie. The projected starter's save % (this season + last, with two seasons ago at half weight), regressed with league-average shots. It scales the player's expected scoring rate by (1 − sv%) / (1 − league sv%).

    The ice-time and matchup parts are backtested on this season's games using only data available before each game; one multiplier (×) lines the forecasts up with how often players actually score. Lines and the goalie factor aren't in the backtest. Power-play units and lines are derived from NHL data, not official.

    Recent form (▲ Hot / ▼ Cold) compares a player's last 10 games' points with what his minutes and long-run rates would produce, shrunk with 120 minutes of long-run scoring. It's shown for context but not weighted: on this season's games, adding it at any weight made forecasts less accurate (Brier 0.2054 with none, 0.2057 at a light weight, 0.2079 at full weight), and shot-volume trends didn't help either. Recent ice time, power-play share and linemates, which are weighted, carry the useful part of a player's current trend.

    Projected starters are a guess (on a back-to-back, the goalie who sat last night; otherwise the one with the most of the last 5 starts). They are not confirmed lineups. "Active" means the player dressed in one of his team's last 3 games.

    Sportsbook odds come from The Odds API (). Each book's Over/Under pair is converted to probabilities and the bookmaker margin removed. Where a book only offers a 1.5-point line, the chance of 1+ point is derived assuming Poisson scoring (marked *). Edge = model minus the books' average. Large edges often mean the model is missing news (injury, line change, role) — check before trusting them. Not betting advice.

    Five-player combinations. Each leg is the model's call for that player: "Point" when P(point) is at least 50%, otherwise "No point". Combined chance multiplies the five legs, which assumes they are independent; legs are spread across teams to limit that. Fair payout is what that chance is worth with no bookmaker margin. A book's parlay price appears only when that one book prices all five legs on the 0.5-point line. Kalshi percentages are the midpoint of each "1+ points" contract's Yes bid and ask (inverted for "no point" legs), refreshed every hour. "At Kalshi prices" estimates a combo return by buying each leg at its ask (Yes ask, or No ask = 1 − Yes bid), before Kalshi fees. Kalshi does not sell these exact five-leg combos, so treat it as a price comparison. Not betting advice.
