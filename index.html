How the agent scores a player

  1. Elo momentum. Every skater carries a point-Elo, updated after each game since (K = ). A point is a "win" against a 1500 benchmark. Elo converts to log-odds directly, so the rating is the base probability, weighted toward recent form.
  2. History vs opponent. The share of games with a point against tonight's opponent, shrunk toward the player's overall rate with pseudo-games and capped at ±0.4 log-odds.
  3. Opposing goalie. The projected starter's save % (this season + last, with two seasons ago at half weight), regressed with league-average shots. It scales the player's expected scoring rate by (1 − sv%) / (1 − league sv%).

Projected starters are a guess (on a back-to-back, the goalie who sat last night; otherwise the one with the most of the last 5 starts). They are not confirmed lineups. "Active" means the player dressed in one of his team's last 3 games.

Sportsbook odds come from The Odds API (). Each book's Over/Under pair is converted to probabilities and the bookmaker margin removed. Where a book only offers a 1.5-point line, the chance of 1+ point is derived assuming Poisson scoring (marked *). Edge = model minus the books' average. Large edges often mean the model is missing news (injury, line change, role) — check before trusting them. Not betting advice.

Five-player combinations. Each leg is the model's call for that player: "Point" when P(point) is at least 50%, otherwise "No point". Combined chance multiplies the five legs, which assumes they are independent; legs are spread across teams to limit that. Fair payout is what that chance is worth with no bookmaker margin. A book's parlay price appears only when that one book prices all five legs on the 0.5-point line. Not betting advice.
