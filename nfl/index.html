How the model works
  • Gradient boosting + quantile regression — two models per stat (receiving yards for RB/WR/TE, rushing yards for RBs), trained on every player-game since 2022. The projection is the average of an XGBoost and a LightGBM regressor (expected yards). The distribution comes from seven LightGBM quantile regressors (5, 10, 25, 50, 75, 90 and 95%). These are kept from crossing, then widened around the median just enough that the 10–90% band covers 80% of outcomes on a held-out season (a conformal adjustment). P(yards > line) is read from that distribution at any line, linearly between quantiles with exponential tails. It is then Platt-calibrated on the same held-out games.
  • Rolling usage, not just raw yards — exponentially weighted (half-life 4 games, carried across seasons), always from games before the one projected. The inputs are snap share, route participation, target share, air-yards share, WOPR, carry share, targets, carries, aDOT, yards per target and per carry, and last-game snaps, routes and shares. Route participation is the share of the team's dropbacks the player was on the field for, from nflverse participation charting. Participation for the current season is published after the season, so this season's games use snap share × the player's own routes-per-snap ratio from his last charted season.
  • Opponent defense, adjusted for strength (DVOA-style) — every defensive game is compared with what that offense normally does. Pass and rush EPA/play allowed minus the offense's own rolling EPA/play, pressure rate (sacks + QB hits per dropback) above the offense's norm, and receiving yards allowed to the player's position (RB/WR/TE), and rushing yards allowed to RBs, as a ratio to the offense's usual output. Each is then rolled over the defense's games (half-life 6). Two-high safety shell rate is the share of dropbacks in Cover 2 / 2-Man / Cover 4 / Cover 6, from participation charting. It carries the defense's latest charted rate when this season's charting isn't out yet.
  • Game script — implied team total (half the game total plus half the spread), point spread, game total, expected plays (team plays per game and plays the opponent allows), neutral-situation seconds per snap, pass rate over expected, home, dome and wind.
  • Feature-group weights — each projection is split into per-feature contributions (SHAP values) and grouped into usage, opponent defense, game script and team offense. By default each group carries the share the model learned. For receiving yards the weights are set by hand: game script 4%, usage 87.4%, and the other two groups split what's left in their learned proportions. That group's contributions are scaled to hit the share, and the projected distribution moves with the projection. The table below shows learned vs applied weights. The accuracy figures are measured with the weights applied.
  • Training & validation — the weekly model is trained on every game before the week shown, so finished games this week (e.g. Thursday night) are true out-of-sample checks in the Result column. Accuracy, interval coverage and calibration below come from a held-out season (the current season's earlier weeks once there are enough of them).
  • Probability line — Best available uses the FanDuel line, then DraftKings, then the Kalshi strike priced closest to 50/50, then the model's own median when no market lists the player. FanDuel/DraftKings lines are pulled once per game, on the first refresh within 30 hours of kickoff (The Odds API). Kalshi is refreshed every run. The ± buttons move a player's line in 5-yard steps, and the change is saved in this browser.

Data: nflverse (play-by-play, participation, snap counts, rosters, injuries, schedules and lines). Odds: FanDuel, DraftKings (The Odds API) and Kalshi public markets. Projections are statistical estimates, not guarantees.
